### 1. The Mathematical Pitfall of Weighting Actions

To see why action-level scaling fails to solve class dominance, look at the objective function that your PPO agent optimizes during a batch update. The clipped policy gradient loss is calculated as an expected value over the entire rollout buffer $D$:

$$L^{CLIP}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_{t \in D} \left[ \min\left(r_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t, \, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_t\right) \right]$$

Because this expectation is pro-rated by the exact frequency of states present in the rollout buffer, the total loss gradient $\nabla_\theta L(\theta)$ is a linear combination of cluster-specific gradients:

$$\nabla_\theta L(\theta) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} P(k) \cdot \nabla_\theta L_k(\theta)$$

Where $P(k)$ is the categorical footprint share of cluster $k$ in your training pipeline (e.g., $P(2) = 0.319$, $P(5) = 0.048$).

If you alter the DSP action vector based on cluster distribution (for example, by scaling down the magnitude or the delta of actions inside a dominant cluster), you change the geometric sensitivity of the environment's response to that cluster. However, **you do not change $P(k)$**.

Cluster 2 still commands nearly $32\%$ of the backpropagation pass. The network's shared inner dense layers will still undergo weight changes optimized primarily to predict values and calculate advantages for Cluster 2, ultimately driving the conditional pathways of minority clusters into catastrophic forgetting.

---

### 2. How Actions *Should* Be Conditioned: Dynamic Noise Scaling

If you want to introduce cluster-conditioned rules directly into the action domain without dropping samples, the correct mathematical approach is to modulate **Action Exploration Variance (Action Noise)** based on the real-time **Attack Success Rate (ASR)** of each cluster, rather than scaling the raw action values by absolute frequency.

In standard continuous PPO, actions are sampled from a Gaussian distribution:

$$a_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_\theta(s_t), \, \sigma^2)$$

Currently, your agent utilizes a global, decaying entropy coefficient that enforces uniform variance across all states. If Cluster 5 has completely cracked the AASIST3 model (High ASR) but Cluster 2 is completely failing (Zero ASR), a uniform variance forces the model to thrash erratically around its winning Cluster 5 coordinates while exploring too timidly to escape the Cluster 2 local minima.

#### The Strategy:

Scale the standard deviation $\sigma$ of your policy's output head inversely to the cluster's real-time evasion rate:

$$\sigma_k = \sigma_{\text{base}} \cdot \left(1.0 - \text{ASR}_k\right) + \sigma_{\text{floor}}$$

* **For Winner Clusters (High ASR):** The variance shrinks. The policy becomes deterministic for that sub-manifold, locking onto and exploiting the known vulnerabilities of the spline boundaries.
* **For Robust Clusters (Low ASR):** The variance expands. The policy steps become highly stochastic and aggressive, forcing the agent to travel far outside its initialization bounds to discover hidden holes.

## Technical Solutions

### Solution 1: Implement a Beta Distribution Policy (Recommended)

Instead of sampling from a Gaussian distribution and forcefully clamping it, sample from a **Beta Distribution**. The Beta distribution is naturally bounded on the interval $[0, 1]$ (which is easily rescaled to $[-1, 1]$ via $2x - 1$).

* **Why it fixes the problem:** It completely avoids the vanishing gradient problem because its probability density functions can naturally place high probability mass right at the boundaries without flattening out the tracking gradient.

### Solution 2: Squashed Gaussian with Change-of-Variables Correction

If you must keep a Gaussian distribution, you cannot simply apply `tanh` to the output without correcting the policy's log-likelihood. You must apply the **change-of-variables formula** to penalize actions as they approach the boundaries:


$$\log \pi(a|s) = \log \mu(x|s) - \sum_{i=1}^{D} \log(1 - \tanh^2(x_i))$$


This forces PPO to understand that the action space becomes extremely tight near the edges, stabilizing the gradient updates.

# Deep-Dive Explanation of the Recommendation

To understand why you must allow the rollout buffer to complete its collection pass before evaluating a distribution shift, we must look at the mechanics of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and how deep reinforcement learning models generalize across segmented environments.

#### 1. The Separation of Rollout and Optimization Phases

PPO operates in two discrete, alternating cycles: the **Rollout Phase** and the **Update (Gradient) Phase**.

* **During the Rollout Phase:** The agent interacts with the environment using a frozen copy of its current policy ($\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$). It collects trajectories (states, actions, log-probabilities, and rewards) and stores them in the `RolloutBuffer` until it reaches `n_steps` (4096 steps per environment worker). **No learning happens during this time.** The parameters of the neural network do not change by a single fraction.
* **During the Update Phase:** Once the buffer hits 4096 steps, the rollout stops. The buffer computes the empirical advantages ($A_t$) and value targets. The network then runs its optimization loop over a specified number of epochs (4) using mini-batches (512). This is the exact moment when backpropagation computes gradients and updates the network weights from $\theta_{\text{old}}$ to $\theta_{\text{new}}$.

If you interrupt training or look at the logs mid-rollout, you are observing actions generated by a static policy that has not yet processed the recent breakthroughs of Clusters 0 and 1. You must wait for the rollout to end and the gradient update to execute to see any change in behavior.

#### 2. Cross-Cluster Latent Generalization (Shared Network Weights)

Your environment partitions audio space into GMM clusters, but your policy network is **one unified model** (except for the final layer if using conditional heads, or completely unified if passing the cluster ID as an input token/embedding).

When the agent discovers the highly specific vector (`tilt: -1.0`, `harmonics: 1.0`) that forces a breakthrough in Cluster 0 and Cluster 1, it triggers a massive reward bonus ($+75.0$). In the subsequent optimization phase, this creates a very large positive advantage profile ($A_t \gg 0$) for those trajectories.

The backpropagation step modifies the shared hidden representations inside the policy network to anchor this successful strategy. Because the hidden layers are shared, the architectural changes driven by Cluster 0 and 1 can warp the policy's default behavior for the resilient domains (Clusters 3 and 9). This is known as **latent space manifold alignment**. We are waiting to see if the policy automatically generalizes this structural vulnerability to the harder clusters without needing to stumble into it blindly through raw stochastic exploration.
